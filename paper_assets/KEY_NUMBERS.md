## Caveats to state in the paper
- **Stride:** all 10 traffic seeds generate the same 16 flows (same flow IDs, src and dst, hence the same ECMP paths; checked by regenerating them); only rates and timing differ. ECMP's stride fabric drops (1,831–19,385 per run, `runs[pattern=stride, strategy=ecmp]`) come from one fixed hash-collision pattern, which every guarded strategy removes. Do not count "stride solved" as independent results.
- **LLM training:** 0 fabric drops under every strategy (`runs[pattern=llm_training]`); all its drops are on host links, so no routing strategy changes it.
- 6 test scenarios: forecast CIs describe scenario-to-scenario variation, not population significance.
- Synthetic simulator, one k = 4 topology, no PFC/ECN/RDMA, no route-installation delay; guard settings (max_changes 4, headroom 0.9, min_improvement 0.15) were not tuned.

## Sensitivity analysis: guarded score, max_changes (`research/guard_sweep_v4.json`, new traffic seeds 4000–4009, paired against ECMP on the same traffic)
- max_changes 2: total -3.70% (paired 95% CI -11,539 to -4,653 drops per run), fabric -17.14%, 34.6 reroutes/run, W/T/L 41/10/9 vs ECMP (`summary.max_changes[max_changes=2]`).
- max_changes 4: total -4.28% (paired 95% CI -12,838 to -6,085 drops per run), fabric -19.50%, 33.9 reroutes/run, W/T/L 43/10/7 vs ECMP (`summary.max_changes[max_changes=4]`).
- max_changes 8: total -4.19% (paired 95% CI -12,615 to -5,913 drops per run), fabric -19.56%, 32.9 reroutes/run, W/T/L 43/10/7 vs ECMP (`summary.max_changes[max_changes=8]`).
- max_changes 16: total -4.19% (paired 95% CI -12,615 to -5,913 drops per run), fabric -19.56%, 32.9 reroutes/run, W/T/L 43/10/7 vs ECMP (`summary.max_changes[max_changes=16]`).
- On this new traffic the benchmark's guard setting (max_changes 4) cuts total drops by 4.28%, vs 5.72% on the benchmark seeds: the gain holds on unseen traffic (CI excludes 0) but its size depends on the traffic sample.
- Raising max_changes above 4 changes any family's total-drop change by at most 0.51 percentage points (`summary.max_changes[*].per_family`): the per-tick change budget is not what limits the guard.

## What the paper can and cannot claim
- Can: a capacity guard turns a simple congestion signal into a 4.9–5.7% total and 19.8–24.8% fabric drop reduction vs ECMP, with 37.9–58.9 reroutes per run against 547 for reactive rerouting (9–14× fewer), and it works with a model-free congestion score (guarded score is the best guarded variant).
- Can: learned per-link temporal models rank congestion better than persistence.
- Cannot: that the GNN (graph convolution) improves forecasting over an LSTM, or that GNN forecasts reduce drops beyond guard-only baselines.
